# Double Gauss

100 mm EFL, f/5, six elements in four groups roughly symmetric about a central stop
(N-SSK2 / N-SK2-F5 cemented / F5-N-SK16 cemented / N-SK16), 14 degrees half field
(28 full), 486.1 / 587.6 / 656.3 nm. `optiland.samples.objectives.DoubleGauss`
unmodified. On-axis RMS spot 11.2 um against a 3.3 um Airy radius — this sample trades
some on-axis correction for the speed and field it covers; do not read the on-axis
number alone as "not as good as the triplet," the working point is different.

Good for: fast, flat-field general-purpose imaging — every normal photographic lens
from f/2 to f/1.4 descends from this form. Near-symmetry about the stop cancels coma
and distortion to first order for free; the real design effort goes into balancing
spherical, astigmatism and Petzval sum across the four (or more, in faster versions)
glass groups. Below about f/1.4 or past about 25 degrees half field, oblique
spherical aberration and zonal astigmatism are what give first, not the aberrations
this form is famous for controlling.

Release the two elements nearest the stop first (they carry most of the spherical
and coma correction), then the outer meniscus elements for field curvature and
astigmatism, and treat the near-perfect front/back symmetry as a constraint worth
preserving unless you have a specific reason to break it.

Kingslake, *A History of the Photographic Lens* (Gauss objectives, Taylor/Rudolph/
Merte lineage); Smith, *Modern Lens Design*, ch. 10.
