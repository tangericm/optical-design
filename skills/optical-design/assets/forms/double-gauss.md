# Double Gauss

100 mm EFL, f/5, six elements in four groups roughly symmetric about a central stop
(N-SSK2 / N-SK2-F5 cemented / F5-N-SK16 cemented / N-SK16), 14 degrees half field,
486.1 / 587.6 / 656.3 nm. `optiland.samples.objectives.DoubleGauss` unmodified.
On-axis RMS spot 11.2 um against a 3.3 um Airy radius — this sample trades on-axis
correction for speed and field; don't read the on-axis number alone against the
triplet, the working point differs.

Good for fast, flat-field general imaging — every normal photographic lens from f/2
to f/1.4 descends from this form. Near-symmetry about the stop cancels coma and
distortion to first order for free; the design effort goes into balancing spherical,
astigmatism and Petzval sum across the glass groups. Below about f/1.4 or past 25
degrees half field, oblique spherical and zonal astigmatism give first.

Release the two elements nearest the stop first (spherical, coma), then the outer
meniscus elements for field curvature and astigmatism, and preserve the front/back
symmetry unless you have a specific reason to break it.

Kingslake, *A History of the Photographic Lens* (Taylor/Rudolph/Merte lineage); Smith,
*Modern Lens Design*, ch. 10.
