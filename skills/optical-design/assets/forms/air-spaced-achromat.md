# Air-spaced achromat

100 mm EFL, f/4, N-BK7 crown and N-SF5 flint with a small air gap (0.84 mm nominal)
between two nearly-matched concave surfaces instead of a cemented interface, 486.1 /
587.6 / 656.3 nm. Same construction method as the cemented doublet — thin-lens power
split, thick-lens EFL rescale, then a wide-bound `LeastSquares` polish on EFL and
on-axis combined-wavelength RMS spot. On-axis RMS spot is 3.8 um against a 2.7 um Airy
radius at f/4 — close to diffraction-limited on-axis, noticeably better than the
cemented form at a faster aperture, which is the point of the extra surface.

Good for: the same narrow-field relay and beam-conditioning roles as the cemented
doublet, one stop faster or one aberration order better corrected, when the assembly
can afford a spacer ring and a fourth surface's tolerance stack instead of cement.
Faster than about f/3.5 spherical dominates again; the extra surface buys you
roughly one additional stop over a cemented doublet before that happens, not two.
Field behavior is essentially identical to the cemented form — coma limits you past a
few degrees half field.

Release the two crown radii together first (they move as a near-matched pair), then
the flint's back radius for color balance, then the air gap itself, which trades
spherical against coma and is the real reason this form outperforms the cemented one.

Kidger, *Fundamental Optical Design*, ch. 4; Sasián, *Introduction to Lens Design*,
ch. 10 (achromatic doublets, cemented vs. air-spaced).
