# Air-spaced achromat

100 mm EFL, f/4, N-BK7 crown and N-SF5 flint with a small air gap (0.84 mm) between
two nearly-matched concave surfaces instead of a cemented interface, 486.1 / 587.6 /
656.3 nm. Same construction as the cemented doublet — power split, thick-lens rescale,
wide-bound `LeastSquares` polish. On-axis RMS spot is 3.8 um against a 2.7 um Airy
radius at f/4 — near diffraction-limited, one stop faster than the cemented form at
similar correction, which is the point of the extra surface.

Good for the same narrow-field relay roles as the cemented doublet, when the assembly
can afford a spacer ring and a fourth surface's tolerance stack. The extra surface
buys roughly one stop over a cemented doublet before spherical dominates again (past
about f/3.5). Field behavior is essentially the same — coma limits past a few degrees.

Release the two crown radii together first (they move as a matched pair), then the
flint back radius for color balance, then the air gap itself — it trades spherical
against coma and is the real reason this form beats the cemented one.

Kidger, *Fundamental Optical Design*, ch. 4; Sasián, *Introduction to Lens Design*,
ch. 10.
