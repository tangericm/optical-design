# Cooke triplet

50 mm EFL, f/5, three elements (SK16 positive / F2 negative / SK16 positive), stop at
the negative element, 20 degrees half field, 480 / 550 / 650 nm.
`optiland.samples.objectives.CookeTriplet` unmodified. On-axis RMS spot 4.3 um against
a 3.4 um Airy radius; grows to about 17 um at 0.7 field and drops back to 13 um at full
field — the classic triplet zonal dip, not monotonic.

Good for the cheapest lens form that genuinely covers a wide field at moderate speed.
Three elements, three airspaces, six curvatures and two glasses give roughly one
variable per Seidel term (spherical, coma, astigmatism, Petzval, distortion, axial and
lateral color) after power and achromatism use up two — why it has stayed in use since
1893. Past about f/4 or 25 degrees half field it runs out of degrees of freedom;
astigmatism and field curvature give first.

Release the two air gaps first (stop position, Petzval balance), then the outer
elements' curvatures, and the negative element's curvatures last — it carries most of
the astigmatism correction and is sensitive.

Kingslake credits Taylor, 1893; Kidger, *Fundamental Optical Design*, ch. 6; Sasián,
*Introduction to Lens Design*, ch. 12.
