# Cemented achromat doublet

50 mm EFL, f/5, N-BK7 crown cemented to N-SF5 flint, one cemented interface, 486.1 /
587.6 / 656.3 nm. Built here from the thin-lens power-split equations (crown power
`phi1 = phi * V1/(V1-V2)`), scaled to the real thick-lens EFL, then polished on-axis
with `LeastSquares` against EFL and combined-wavelength RMS spot. On-axis RMS spot is
5.3 um against a 3.3 um Airy radius at 587.6 nm — a couple of waves of residual
spherical, typical for an off-the-shelf catalog achromat rather than a hand-optimized
one.

Good for: collimating or focusing a narrow beam (laser coupling, finder optics, a
single relay element) where you need color correction but not a wide field. Past about
f/4 spherical aberration grows faster than you can bend it away with two glasses; past
about 3-4 degrees half field, coma takes over and no amount of rebending the same two
elements fixes both at once — that is what the triplet and Tessar forms below are for.

Aberrations, in the order you will hit them pushing this form harder: spherical (stop
down or accept it), coma (limits field), secondary spectrum (the visible-band
color you cannot remove with two glasses — irreducible without an ED or fluorite
element). Release the front and cemented radii first when optimizing (bending), the
flint back radius second to trim residual color balance, and only touch center
thicknesses last since they barely move any aberration here.

Kidger, *Fundamental Optical Design*, ch. 4 (achromatic doublets); Smith, *Modern Lens
Design*, ch. 4.
