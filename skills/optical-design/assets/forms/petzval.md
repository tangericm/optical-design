# Petzval lens

50.6 mm EFL, f/1.4, four elements in two widely separated groups (N-LAK12/SF4 cemented
front pair, long air gap, separated N-LAK12 / LF5 rear pair), 7 degrees half field,
486.1 / 587.6 / 656.3 nm. `optiland.samples.objectives.PetzvalLens` unmodified.
On-axis RMS spot 96.7 um against a 0.95 um Airy radius at f/1.4 — deliberately fast
and narrow-field; don't expect a clean on-axis spot from this form the way you would
from a slower lens.

Good for speed over field — projection lenses, fast relays, the historical
portrait-lens role. Two widely spaced achromatic-ish groups push aperture much further
than a triplet or Double Gauss at the same element count, at the direct cost of field:
the namesake Petzval field curvature is essentially unconstrained by this topology, so
useful field stays under about 10 degrees half field without a field-flattener near
the image.

Release the rear group's air gap first (focus, field curvature), then the front
cemented pair for spherical; the wide central gap is what makes this form fast and
what limits its field.

Kingslake, *A History of the Photographic Lens* (Petzval, 1840); Sasián, *Introduction
to Lens Design*, ch. 13.
