# Telephoto

127.0 mm EFL, f/5.6, five elements (N-BK7/N-BASF2 front group, air gap, N-ZK7/N-LAF33/
SF1 rear group), 10 degrees half field, 486.1 / 587.6 / 656.3 nm.
`optiland.samples.objectives.Telephoto` unmodified. Total track is 106.1 mm for a
127.0 mm EFL — a telephoto ratio (track/EFL) around 0.84, noticeably shorter than a
"normal" lens of the same focal length would be, which is the entire reason this form
exists. On-axis RMS spot 10.9 um against a 4.0 um Airy radius at f/5.6.

Good for: any application where focal length is fixed by the application (reach, or a
required image scale) but physical length is constrained — long-lens photography,
compact long-focus relays, satellite and surveillance optics. A telephoto is a positive
front group and a negative rear group separated by most of the system's focal length;
the negative group's divergence is what compresses the physical package relative to
the focal length. Push the telephoto ratio much below about 0.7 and the rear negative
group's aberration contributions (particularly lateral color and coma) grow faster
than a fixed element count can absorb.

Release the air gap between the two groups first — it is the primary telephoto-ratio
and focus control — then the front group's curvatures for spherical and color, and the
rear group's curvatures last since they carry most of the field-dependent aberrations
and are the most sensitive to small changes.

Kingslake, *A History of the Photographic Lens*, ch. 8 (telephoto lenses); Smith,
*Modern Lens Design*, ch. 8.
